What you should know before spending anything

  • Certain

    It has never touched a real slab. Everything below was checked in CAD and in a simulator. Real PSA label payloads, real glare and real cup behaviour are still unknown. The label formats were checked on public scans of 64 real slabs, but not through the machine's own camera and plastic. That is why the build starts with two small tests, about $470 for the cameras (about $280 with a borrowed Pi) and $155 for the cup, before any motion parts are bought.

  • Likely

    Nothing like it is for sale. No product takes 200–300 slabs and returns cert numbers unattended. Card sorters exclude slabs, and sheet-fed scanners can't take them.

  • Likely

    A $350–650 hands-free 2D scanner gets you most of the way with no build. Fed by hand it reads 20–30 slabs a minute, about 10 minutes per 250. The machine buys back those minutes and adds a photo of every label, which helps catch fakes and swapped cards. Loading and unloading still take you about 10 minutes either way.

  • Likely

    The budget fits, with headroom. The full list is about $2,182 with a 15% allowance for shipping and duties. A realistic worst case (tools bought new, the optional parts, guessed prices 20% high) is about $2,380, inside the $2,500–3,000 you said is fine. With the school's filament, wire and screws it is about $1,862. Spend the headroom only when a test asks for it.

  • Likely

    The cup never touches a slab it hasn't proven face-down. A PSA back QR or a CGC back code proves it. Older slabs with no back code pass when their card back matches backs the machine already confirmed; a face-up slab stops untouched. In the simulator 2 of 330 slabs stopped for you to confirm. The card-back match is tuned in Phase 0 on your own slabs, so until then treat it as Guessing.

  • Certain

    It is a real mechatronics project: about 60–85 hours of hands-on work plus printing, with the instructor needed for the 120 V wiring, crimp review, squaring the frame and the first power-up.

The bed, left to right. 13 pockets of 84 × 139 mm inside 8 mm dowels, 92 mm apart, and the upward camera in the middle cell.

  1. Load face-down, label toward you.Up to 30 slabs in each of pockets 1–11, below the MAX line on the fill gauge. Pockets 0 and 12 stay empty.
  2. The cup lifts the top slab straight up.A 25 mm non-marking cup on a spring buffer touches only the slab's back. No slab ever slides on another.
  3. The head camera reads the back of the labelThe QR on 2017+ labels, the barcode on 2016+ labels. The frame is cropped to the slab under the cup so a neighbour's label can't be read by mistake.
  4. The slab goes onto the next pocket to the left.Stack 1 rebuilds itself in pocket 0, stack 2 in pocket 1, and so on, so every slab in the load is read, not just the top layer.
  5. Unread backs are held over the upward camerafor the front label. Anything still unread, or from BGS, SGC or TAG, goes to pocket 12 for you to check.
The machine

One beam, one head, two motors

Footprintabout 1.42 × 0.32 m, 0.65 m tall; fits a 6 ft bench
MotionX: GT2 belt on a 1300 mm MGN12 rail on a 4040 beam. Z: NEMA 17 with a T8×8 lead screw on an MGN12 rail.
GripperSMC ZPT25UUK20-B5-A10: 25 mm urethane cup on a non-rotating 20 mm spring buffer, optical contact sensor with an anti-rotation flag guide, normally-closed valves, check valve and reservoir
CamerasUSB 8 MP head camera 140 mm above the slab back, 5 mm toward the label so it also sees the card back; Pi Camera Module 3 140 mm below the front at the station; ring lights on the lens axis to keep glare off the label
ControllerFluidNC on a BTT Rodent, Raspberry Pi 5 running the Python software
Travel heightsempty 248 mm, loaded 248 mm above the pad: a double pick of two thick holders clears the 218 mm dowels by 5 mm even with the cup hanging free; a second optical sensor confirms the head really reached carry height before every sideways move
GradersPSA and CGC are read. BGS and TAG are set aside, and fit only if they measure ≤ 82.6 mm wide. SGC (about 86 mm) does not fit the pockets.
Touches the slabthe cup on the back only; edges against wooden dowels; slabs rest on each other as in any stack
Outputcerts.csv (cert, grader, status, pocket, position), certs.txt, report.html with a photo of each label

Verification

What has been checked, and what hasn't

  • CAD

    177/177 geometric checks pass (cad/checks.py): every pick and travel move, all parts against each other, the held slab up to the hard top stop, both camera lines of sight, the card-back window against 22 real PSA and 27 real CGC backs, the probe flag and buffer stroke, the carry-height sensor, both limit switches, and every printed part a single solid.

  • Simulator

    299 software tests pass. The simulator raises a crash on any unsafe move. They cover full batches, real PSA and CGC payload strings, double picks, thick holders, face-up and neighbour stops, a Z stall, a mis-aimed camera, vacuum leaks, sensor faults, E-stop and resume.

  • Real labels

    Public scans of 64 real PSA and CGC slabs, rescaled to the machine's cameras: 60 of 61 read confidently, 0 wrong certs, 0 false face-down proofs. The rules were written from these same slabs, and studio scans are easier than the machine's camera through plastic.

  • Not tested

    Your own slabs through the machine's camera, real glare off the plastic, cup marking over hundreds of cycles, belt tension, cable routing, the probe flag, and the real shapes and loads of bought parts. Phase 0 and the acceptance tests in the build guide exist to find these out.

Build order

Two go / no-go gates before the big order

  1. Phase 0A: camera testPrint the jig, set up the Pi, scan 30–50 of your own slabs with python -m tools.phase0.5–6 h + about 14 h printing · about $470 (about $280 with a borrowed Pi) · go only if ≥ 95% read and 0 wrong
  2. Phase 0B: cup bench testThe real cup and buffer on a hand vacuum pump: does it seal on your holders, and does it leave a mark after 24 hours?3–4 h + a 24 h test · about $155 · no mains wiring · go only if it seals and leaves no mark
  3. Order and printOrder the machine parts from the BOM, print the parts in cad/out/printed/.1–4 weeks delivery · about 26 h printing
  4. BuildFrame, bed, X axis, Z axis and head, plumbing, electronics, firmware, software. The machine drills its own rod holes through a printed template.about 40–55 h · instructor for squaring, mains and first power-up
  5. Calibrate and run the acceptance tests on junk slabs100-cycle dry run, 300-cycle marking test, drop test, power-cut test, double picks, sensor faults, E-stop and resume.about 8–11 h over 3 days
  6. First real batch, supervisedStay with it the first time and check every cert in certs.csv against the slabs.about 2 h
